Belvantage — a shared loyalty card for neighbourhood shops

A simple observation: a small independent shop has neither the budget nor the time to build its own loyalty app, and its customers end up with a stack of paper cards they keep losing. Belvantage offers a single shared app — every customer collects points across several shops, and every shopkeeper scans a QR code to award points, without building anything themselves.

The approach

From framing to code, with one decision that changed everything along the way

01

Framing

The product doesn't belong to any one shop: it's a shared platform built to welcome any local business — bakery, café, salon, craftsperson. Two profiles with opposite needs: the customer who wants to collect points without hassle, and the shopkeeper who wants loyal customers without building anything.

02

The QR code pivot

The first version of the validation flow relied only on a 4-digit code read out by the shopkeeper — functional, but with friction. A benchmark of Starbucks Rewards confirmed that a "scan the QR, points are added automatically" mechanism reduced that friction for everyone, not just tech-savvy users. The QR code became the main flow; the 4-digit code remains available as a fallback and was never removed.

The real QR code, generated on the customer side — everyone has a unique, permanent code, modelled on a Basic-Fit membership card.

03

Two flows, one design system

Instead of two separate projects, a single Design System (colours, typography, components) powers both experiences. Above all, the shopkeeper dashboard had to feel credible to a real independent business owner: 7/30/90-day filters with genuinely different data per period, and the main action ("Scan a customer") front and centre rather than hidden in a menu.

04

A real dark mode, not a reskin

First naive attempt: duplicate every screen in a dark version — quickly unmanageable and not the proper method. The right solution was Figma's built-in mechanism (SET_VARIABLE_MODE): one set of screens, one switch that instantly flips every colour bound to variables — no duplication. Reproduced identically in code with a single global CSS class.

Real dark mode — background and surfaces follow Material Design guidelines (surfaces lighter than the background, never pure black).

05

From Figma to working code

Once the Figma prototype was finished, the project moved to plain HTML/CSS/JS — deliberately without a framework, so it stays readable and editable without complex tooling. The QR code and the shopkeeper's camera scanner really work: browser camera access, real-time image decoding, all in native JavaScript.

The scanner uses the browser's real camera (getUserMedia) — not a visual mock-up.

Research & benchmark

Honest research rather than a fake survey

With no real client or user panel for this personal project, the research relies on public secondary data and a genuine competitive benchmark — not on made-up statistics.

Secondary data

Fidme Barometer 2024

A household owns 16 loyalty cards on average but only uses 2 a week — the starting point that justifies a shared app rather than yet another card.

Direct competitor

Fidme

Digitises loyalty cards that already exist. Doesn't solve the problem of a shopkeeper who has no digital programme to digitise — the blind spot this project fills.

Inspiration from another sector

Too Good To Go

Frictionless, location-based discovery, borrowed for the project's Discover screen.

Interaction design

Micro-interactions that were tested, not just drawn

A systematic audit of the prototype uncovered real defects before they reached the code: buttons leading nowhere, screens with no way back, sensitive actions with no confirmation.

Confirmation before a sensitive action

Redeeming a reward or logging out happened in a single tap — a cancellable confirmation step was added to both.

What's next

Next step: a real database

The code runs on dummy data stored in the browser — every QR code ID is unique and permanent, but nothing is shared between devices yet. The plan: connect a real database (Supabase) for real user accounts, and add the network error screens already designed in the Figma prototype but not yet coded.

A concept open for discussion

Belvantage is a personal project — a complete concept, from Figma prototype to working code, but it doesn't belong to any shop and isn't (yet) a launched commercial product. If you're a shop, a company or an entrepreneur interested in this idea — takeover, licensing, or a collaboration to develop it together — feel free to get in touch to discuss it.
