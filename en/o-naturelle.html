Ô Natur'Elle — a complete prototype for a beauty salon

Ô Natur'Elle is a premium beauty salon founded by Laetitia Van Driessche, a qualified beautician based in Wemmel, Belgium. For this client project, I designed a complete, responsive Figma prototype from start to finish — with one simple guiding idea: help an undecided client find the right treatment and book it effortlessly.

The approach

Five steps, from first sketch to clickable prototype

01

Framing

A premium, warm positioning, designed mobile-first: most clients of a beauty salon book from their phone, between two appointments. The site architecture starts from that observation rather than the other way round.

02

The beauty diagnosis

The core feature of the project: a 4-step questionnaire (area to treat, availability, budget) that automatically recommends the most suitable treatment. The goal is to reduce indecision — often the real reason a visitor leaves this kind of site without booking.

Diagnosis result: a clear recommendation, justified by the client's answers.

03

Booking

A 3-step flow with a progress indicator visible at all times, a loading screen while the time slot is checked, then an animated confirmation (tick + confetti) — turning a simple appointment into a pleasant moment rather than an admin form.

Step 1 of 3: every step shows progress, never one endless form all at once.

04

Catalogue & shop

Twelve treatments sorted by category (hair removal, face, body, nails), each with price and duration shown right on the thumbnail, plus search and filters. On top of that, a five-product shop with quick view, and a gallery with a photo viewer.

The full treatment menu — search, category filters, price and duration visible immediately.

05

Desktop version

The whole flow was redesigned for large screens rather than simply scaled up: a two-column layout on the home page, error states for the contact form, and legal pages (legal notice, privacy policy) plus a 404 page, for a truly complete prototype.

Planned test protocol for Ô Natur'Elle

  1. Goal: check that an undecided client can get from the beauty diagnosis to a confirmed booking without getting stuck.
  2. Participants: 5 people, including regular beauty salon clients.
  3. Method: remote moderated test on the Figma prototype, think-aloud, diagnosis → booking flow.
  4. Metrics: completion rate, time per step, perceived clarity of the diagnosis recommendation.
  5. Reporting: a prioritised list of pain points, before moving to development.

Structuring the experience

User flow & information architecture

The questionnaire revealed two distinct behaviours: some clients know right away which treatment to book, others hesitate and need guidance. So two flows were designed, both leading to the same booking.

Direct flow
Home→ Treatments→ Treatment page→ Booking→ Confirmation
Guided flow
Home→ Diagnosis→ 4 questions→ Recommendation→ Booking→ Confirmation

When a user finishes the diagnosis, she goes straight to step 2 of the booking (date and time) without choosing the treatment again — the diagnosis has already done that step for her.

Why this structure? Two paths lead to booking: a direct one for clients who already know which treatment they want, and a guided one through the Diagnosis for those who hesitate — the most common case according to the questionnaire.

The Simulator stays on the home page rather than on a separate page: it's a discovery tool, not a step in the booking flow. Products, Gallery and Contact remain accessible from the main navigation, without ever interrupting the booking flow.
